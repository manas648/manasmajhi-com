When I talk about Odisha to people who don't know it, I am not doing regional promotion. I am describing a place that has shaped how I think about almost everything that matters to me — opportunity, systems, what gets built where and why, who benefits and who doesn't.

I grew up in Kalahandi. I built companies from this state. The question of why Odisha matters is not abstract to me.

>> What matters about Odisha is not its size, its tourism, or its minerals. It is what it demonstrates about what is possible when a place is taken seriously.

## The historical claim

Odisha has one of the oldest and most distinctive civilisational identities in the Indian subcontinent. The Kalinga empire, which controlled much of the eastern coast of India and projected maritime power across Southeast Asia, was centred here. The Kalinga War in 261 BCE — the battle that transformed Ashoka's imperial ambitions into one of history's most significant ethical reckonings — was fought here. The temples of Odisha's Golden Triangle (Bhubaneswar, Puri, Konark) represent one of the most coherent concentrations of pre-medieval Hindu temple architecture anywhere in the world.

This historical depth matters because it shapes the identity of a place. Odisha is not a colonial construction or an administrative convenience. It is a civilisation with a long memory of its own significance. That self-understanding is part of why Odia people tend to carry a quiet confidence about where they're from — even when the rest of India hasn't caught up to it.

## The resource position

India's economic development increasingly runs on Odisha's endowment. The state holds approximately 34% of India's iron ore reserves, 98% of its chromite, and significant proportions of its coal, bauxite, manganese, and nickel. The steel industry that supplies Indian manufacturing — and increasingly, global markets — is substantially dependent on Odisha's mineral base.

This resource position would matter in any era. In an era where infrastructure buildout, renewable energy manufacturing, and global supply chain diversification are placing unprecedented demand on raw materials, it matters more. The port infrastructure at Paradip and Dhamra is positioned to export this endowment at scale. The Odisha government has been deliberately building the policy and physical infrastructure to capture more of the value chain rather than exporting raw materials at the bottom of the value curve.

## The coastal position

India's coastline is an asset that the country is only beginning to use at its potential. Odisha's 480-kilometre coast gives it access to Bay of Bengal trade routes that connect eastern India to Southeast Asia, East Africa, and the Gulf. Paradip Port is already one of India's largest. The Sagarmala initiative — the national program to develop port-led industrialisation — has significant Odisha investments in its current phase.

The eastern coast of India has historically been undersourced relative to the western coast. That gap is closing. When it does, the states with significant coastal infrastructure and manufacturing capacity will be positioned differently than they are today. Odisha is building toward that position.

## The talent output

KIIT University in Bhubaneswar is one of India's largest private universities by enrollment. IIT Bhubaneswar, NIT Rourkela, and IIIT Bhubaneswar add significant technical institution density. The state produces a large volume of engineering and technical graduates annually — more than the current local economy fully absorbs, which means much of this talent has been dispersed to Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and beyond.

That dispersal is not entirely a loss. It means there is a substantial Odia diaspora in Indian and global technology companies — engineers, managers, and founders who carry a connection to the state and who represent a potential resource for companies willing to invest in recruiting from and building in Odisha.

## Why now

The convergence of several trends is making Odisha more relevant, faster, than at any point in recent decades. Industrial investment is accelerating — Tata Steel, JSW, POSCO, and other major manufacturers have significant Odisha operations. The port infrastructure is expanding. The government's investor-friendly positioning has improved the business environment. The Smart City investment in Bhubaneswar is producing visible urban quality improvements.

The state is not there yet. The gap between what Odisha has and what it has made of what it has is still real and significant. But the direction is clearer than it has been, and the inputs for a different trajectory are in place in a way they weren't a generation ago.

Odisha matters because of what it has always been. It is beginning to matter for what it is becoming.
