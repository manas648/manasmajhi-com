Gujarat is the standard against which Indian states are benchmarked when the conversation is about industrial development, investor attraction, and business-friendly governance. The "Gujarat model" — a combination of infrastructure investment, government efficiency, proactive investor relations, and policy consistency — is widely credited with the state's economic success and has been held up as a template for other states to follow.

Odisha is one of the states that has explicitly studied the Gujarat model and attempted to adapt elements of it. The Make in Odisha conclave, modelled partly on the Vibrant Gujarat summit, is the most visible expression of this. The Ease of Doing Business improvements Odisha has made are a sustained effort to close the governance gap.

Comparing the two states fairly — rather than simply measuring Odisha against Gujarat's outcomes — requires looking at what each started with, what each has built, and where the comparison is actually informative.

## What Gujarat built

Gujarat's industrial success rests on several foundations that were, to a significant degree, already present before the "Gujarat model" was articulated as a policy program.

The trading culture of Gujarat — particularly among the Patidars, Banias, and Jains who have dominated Gujarati commerce for centuries — provided an entrepreneurial energy and a risk appetite that states with different social histories don't have in the same form. Business is normalised in Gujarat in a way that is culturally embedded rather than policy-created.

The diaspora is enormous and globally distributed. Gujarati entrepreneurs and professionals in the UK, the US, East Africa, and Southeast Asia represent a capital and network base that has fuelled investment in the state across generations. The diaspora's willingness to invest in Gujarat — and to promote it in their adopted countries — is a powerful informal investment promotion infrastructure.

The port infrastructure on the Gulf of Khambhat and the Saurashtra coast — particularly Mundra, developed by Adani, and the state's other major ports — gives Gujarat one of India's largest and most efficient maritime trade capacities. This was partly a natural endowment and partly deliberate investment, but the scale is significant.

The chemical and petrochemical industry in south Gujarat, centred around Surat and Vadodara, is one of the world's significant chemical manufacturing clusters. The diamond cutting and polishing industry in Surat handles the majority of the world's rough diamonds. These are concentrated, high-value industries with deep supply chains.

## What Odisha has that Gujarat doesn't

**Mineral endowment.** Gujarat has some mineral resources, but not in the concentration or diversity that Odisha has. Odisha's iron ore, chromite, bauxite, coal, and nickel reserves are among the country's largest and most significant. In a world where supply chain security for raw materials is an increasing concern, this is an advantage that Gujarat cannot replicate.

**Deep water ports on the east coast.** Gujarat's port advantages are on the west coast. Odisha's Dhamra Port, with one of the deepest draughts on the east coast, and Paradip Port, handling large volumes of bulk cargo, position Odisha for trade flows to Southeast Asia, East Africa, and the Gulf through the Bay of Bengal. As India's eastern trade connections grow, this geography matters.

**Forest and ecological resources.** Odisha's forest cover, tribal ecological knowledge, and biodiversity assets — including significant wildlife sanctuaries and one of India's largest mangrove ecosystems — are potential economic assets (in sustainable forestry, eco-tourism, and bio-diversity preservation) that Gujarat's more arid geography doesn't have in comparable form.

## Where Gujarat is ahead

**Policy consistency and investor confidence.** Gujarat has maintained a relatively consistent policy environment across multiple government administrations. Investors in Gujarat know what to expect. Odisha has improved significantly, but the track record of consistency is shorter.

**SME and MSME ecosystem.** Gujarat's small and medium enterprise base is extremely large and diverse, providing supply chains, employment, and economic activity across the state rather than concentrated in a few industrial hubs. Odisha's MSME sector is smaller and less developed relative to the state's size.

**Entrepreneurship culture.** The normalisation of business and entrepreneurship in Gujarat society is deeper and more culturally embedded than in Odisha. Changing this requires time and generational shifts in the signal value of different careers.

**Urban economic diversity.** Ahmedabad, Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot — Gujarat has multiple cities with significant economic activity and different industrial specialisations. Odisha's economic activity is more concentrated in Bhubaneswar-Cuttack and the mineral belt industrial clusters, with weaker secondary city economies.

## The honest comparison

Odisha and Gujarat are both industrial states with genuine assets, developing on different timescales and from different starting points. Gujarat's head start — in entrepreneurship culture, diaspora capital, port development, and policy consistency — is real and significant.

Odisha's advantages — in mineral endowment, east coast strategic position, and the specific industries that depend on those inputs — are equally real. As India's economic centre of gravity shifts more toward eastern trade connections and as the demand for the raw materials and primary manufacturing that Odisha excels in grows, the relative advantages change.

Odisha is not trying to become Gujarat. The smarter aspiration is to become the best version of what Odisha can be — an industrial state with world-class port infrastructure, a strong manufacturing base from minerals to finished goods, and a growing knowledge economy layered on top of it. That is a distinctive and valuable position, and the state is moving toward it at a pace that most observers have underestimated.
