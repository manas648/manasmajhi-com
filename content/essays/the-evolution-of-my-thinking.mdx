If I could sit down with my younger self, I suspect we would agree on the important things.
Work hard.
Stay honest.
Keep learning.
Treat people well.
Create value.
But we would disagree on many other things.
Not because one version is right and the other is wrong.
Because experience changes perspective.
And perspective changes thinking.
The older I get, the less interested I become in defending old ideas simply because they are familiar.
I would rather update my understanding of reality than preserve an outdated belief.
That mindset has changed how I see the world.
When I was younger, I viewed success primarily through the lens of effort.
Work harder.
Learn more.
Stay disciplined.
Eventually, results would follow.
There is truth in that idea.
Effort matters.
Discipline matters.
1

Persistence matters.
But reality is more complicated.
Over time, I discovered that opportunity matters too.
Access matters.
Timing matters.
Networks matter.
Systems matter.
The world is not simply a meritocracy.
Nor is it purely a game of luck.
It is a combination of capability, opportunity, and timing.
Recognizing this made my thinking more nuanced.
It also made me more interested in creating opportunities for others.
Because talent without opportunity is one of the greatest wastes imaginable.
Another shift occurred in how I think about people.
Earlier in life, I was fascinated by individuals.
Exceptional performers.
Successful entrepreneurs.
Influential leaders.
Remarkable stories.
I still admire individuals.
But increasingly, I pay attention to systems.
Because systems determine what becomes possible at scale.
A talented individual can change a life.
2

A well-designed system can change millions.
Education systems.
Transportation systems.
Hiring systems.
Economic systems.
Digital systems.
The larger the problem, the more important systems become.
This realization influenced how I think about business.
And ultimately, how I think about impact.
My relationship with geography changed as well.
Growing up, certain places seemed to possess all the opportunities.
Major cities.
Global hubs.
Economic centers.
Everything important appeared to happen there.
Technology challenged that assumption.
The internet reduced distance.
Remote work reduced barriers.
Digital platforms expanded access.
Suddenly, geography mattered less than it once did.
Not because location became irrelevant.
Because access became more distributed.
That shift was profound.
3

It transformed how I thought about Odisha.
About India.
About global opportunity.
About what was possible.
Another major evolution involved power.
For a long time, I paid very little attention to it.
I was more interested in work than influence.
More interested in building than status.
Then life exposed me to worlds I previously knew little about.
Elite networks.
Institutional power.
Influence.
Access.
Decision-making.
At first, it felt foreign.
Eventually, it became understandable.
And understanding changed my perspective.
I no longer see power as something to worship or fear.
I see it as something to understand.
Because power influences outcomes.
The people who ignore it often misunderstand reality.
The people who obsess over it often misunderstand themselves.
The goal is neither admiration nor avoidance.
4

The goal is awareness.
Perhaps the most important change has been my relationship with certainty.
When I was younger, I wanted answers.
Clear answers.
Confident answers.
Definitive answers.
Experience slowly weakened that desire.
Not because answers are unimportant.
Because reality is complex.
Many important questions do not have permanent answers.
They have evolving answers.
The best thinkers I have encountered are rarely the most certain.
They are often the most curious.
The most adaptable.
The most willing to update their beliefs.
That observation changed how I learn.
Today, I value questions more than certainty.
Questions create exploration.
Exploration creates understanding.
Understanding creates better decisions.
The process matters.
Looking back, I realize that my thinking evolved in a consistent direction.
From individuals to systems.
5

From effort to opportunity.
From certainty to curiosity.
From local perspectives to global perspectives.
From short-term outcomes to long-term compounding.
From success to contribution.
Not because I consciously planned it.
Because experience gradually pushed me there.
I suspect this process will continue.
The ideas I hold today will evolve.
Some will strengthen.
Others will change.
A few will disappear entirely.
That is how growth works.
The goal is not to reach a final philosophy.
The goal is to remain open enough to keep refining it.
Because every year adds new experiences.
Every experience adds new perspective.
And every perspective adds another layer to understanding.
When I look at my life today, I do not see a finished worldview.
I see a work in progress.
Which is fitting.
Because the most valuable things in life rarely arrive complete.
They evolve.
6

Just as people do.
Learning Outcome
Growth is not merely the accumulation of knowledge. It is the continuous refinement of perspective. The
willingness to evolve your thinking is often more valuable than the certainty of any single belief.
