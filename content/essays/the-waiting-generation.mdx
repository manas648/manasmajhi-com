On a desk in Odisha, an admit card sits inside a plastic sleeve.

It has sat there for a while.

The sleeve is meant to protect it from dust, from damp, from time.

Everything about the card is precise.

A roll number.

A photograph.

An official seal.

Everywhere the card should carry a date, it carries four words instead.

*To be announced.*

The card says nothing.

It doesn't have to.

Every postponement asks the candidate to keep believing anyway.

---

There is one decade that quietly determines the course of most people's lives.

Your twenties.

It is the decade of first salaries.

First responsibilities.

First acts of independence.

The decade when parents slowly stop worrying because their children have begun standing on their own feet.

Every society shapes this decade differently.

Some fill it with opportunity.

Others fill it with waiting.

>> A society is judged not only by the opportunities it creates, but by how long it asks its young people to wait for them.

---

Somewhere in Odisha tonight, thousands of young people will wake before sunrise.

Not because an examination is tomorrow.

But because it might be.

They will open the same books.

Sit at the same library desks.

Refresh the same recruitment websites.

Answer the same question from family and friends.

*"Any updates?"*

*"Not yet."*

Nothing dramatic happens.

That is precisely the tragedy.

Waiting rarely arrives as a single event.

It accumulates quietly until it begins to feel normal.

---

Having spent years in recruitment, I have come to believe that recruitment has very little to do with jobs.

It has everything to do with time.

The right opportunity arriving at the right moment changes everything.

A graduate becomes a professional.

A professional supports ageing parents, educates younger siblings, builds a home, and eventually starts a family.

One opportunity rarely changes just one life.

Delay it, and everything behind it moves with it.

>> Governments often think they are managing examinations. In reality, they are managing time.

---

Recruitment delays are measured differently by different people.

Administrators count them in months.

Aspirants count them differently.

Somewhere, a wall calendar keeps its own record.

April.

December.

May.

December again.

May again.

Each month crossed out in red.

Each cross meaning the same word, typed in the same official font.

*Postponed.*

The calendar is no longer marking days.

It is marking years.

Festivals spent saying, *"Maybe next year."*

Responsibilities postponed.

Dreams deferred.

Years that never return.

>> A postponed examination is not a delayed event. It is a delayed beginning.

---

Working in recruitment has taught me something else.

Private companies and governments do not hire in the same way.

If a company delays hiring, candidates usually have alternatives.

Another employer.

Another city.

Another opportunity.

Government recruitment is different.

A candidate preparing for OPSC cannot simply find another OPSC.

Someone preparing for OSSSC cannot replace years of preparation with another examination next month.

For many, there is no backup plan.

That notification is not one opportunity among many.

It is the opportunity around which they have organised their lives.

It is the card that never leaves the plastic sleeve.

>> A government vacancy is never just another vacancy. For someone, it is the future they have been preparing for since graduation.

---

What exactly is a paper leak?

Not merely a security lapse.

A breach of trust.

What exactly is a textbook filled with avoidable errors?

Not merely poor proofreading.

A breach of trust.

What exactly is an examination postponed without certainty?

Another breach of trust.

Recruitment boards, examination authorities, and textbook boards may appear unrelated.

Yet they all perform the same function.

They ask citizens to trust them.

Every mistake weakens that trust.

Every repeated mistake weakens it further.

>> Every institution teaches something. Even when it fails.

---

Odisha has no shortage of talent.

Spend a day speaking to students in Bhubaneswar, Rourkela, Berhampur, Sambalpur, Balasore, Koraput, or Bhawanipatna.

You will meet determination.

Discipline.

Resilience.

Young people willing to compete.

They are simply asking for the competition to be fair.

---

We often celebrate physical infrastructure.

Roads.

Bridges.

Airports.

Factories.

They deserve celebration.

But there is another kind of infrastructure that shapes a society just as profoundly.

Institutional infrastructure.

The systems that ensure examinations happen when promised.

Recruitments conclude without unnecessary delay.

Question papers remain secure.

Textbooks deserve the trust children place in them.

These institutions rarely appear in inauguration photographs.

Yet they quietly determine whether an entire generation continues to believe that effort is still rewarded.

>> The strongest institutions are the ones people never have to worry about.

---

This is not about one recruitment board.

Or one department.

Or one government.

Democracies are designed to change governments.

Institutions are designed to outlast them.

Which is why one promise must remain constant.

Every young person preparing for a public examination deserves the same assurance.

A fair process.

A predictable timeline.

An institution worthy of their trust.

That promise should never depend on who happens to be in power.

It should be the minimum standard of governance.

---

There is an old saying.

Justice delayed is justice denied.

Perhaps our generation needs another.

**Opportunity delayed is opportunity diminished.**

Not because ambition disappears.

But because time does.

Roads can be widened.

Airports expanded.

Factories modernised.

Policies rewritten.

But a twenty-four-year-old gets only one chance to be twenty-four.

>> Governments can postpone examinations. They should never postpone a generation.

---

The admit card is still in its plastic sleeve.

The sleeve has done its job.

Nothing has faded.

Nothing has torn.

Only the date remains unwritten.

*To be announced.*

The admit card is ready.

The candidate is ready.

The question is whether the institutions are.
