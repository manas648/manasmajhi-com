When I describe Odisha's economy to people outside the state, the version in their head is usually a decade out of date. Natural resources, steel, government jobs. That picture is real and still accurate as a foundation. What it misses is everything that has been building on top of it.

Odisha's economic identity has historically been defined by natural resources — iron ore, bauxite, chromite, and coal — and the steel industry that processes them. This identity is real and remains the backbone of the state's economy. It is also increasingly incomplete.

The industries growing in Odisha today span a broader range than the state's historic profile suggests.

## Steel and metals — still dominant, now larger

Odisha produces approximately half of India's steel. Rourkela Steel Plant, one of India's oldest integrated steel plants, anchors the western part of the state. Tata Steel, JSW Steel, and JSPL all have significant operations in Odisha. The state's natural resource endowment — the combination of iron ore, coal, and water — creates a structural cost advantage for steel production that is difficult to replicate.

This industry is not declining. It is expanding, driven by India's infrastructure investment and manufacturing ambitions. The capacity additions planned across Odisha's steel plants over the next decade represent significant investment and employment. The steel industry is Odisha's most durable economic advantage and is likely to remain so.

## Ports and logistics — rapidly expanding

Paradip Port is one of India's major ports by cargo volume and has been expanding significantly. Dhamra Port, operated by the Adani Group, is one of India's deepest natural draft ports and is positioned to handle bulk cargo at a scale that most Indian ports cannot. Gopalpur Port is developing as a specialised port for certain industrial cargo.

The combination of coastline, natural harbours, and proximity to India's steel and mineral production makes Odisha increasingly significant to India's trade infrastructure. As India's export ambitions grow — in steel, in manufactured goods, in agricultural products — Odisha's ports are well-positioned to handle a growing share of that volume.

The logistics industry that supports port operations — warehousing, inland transportation, freight forwarding — is growing alongside the ports themselves.

## Pharmaceuticals and chemicals — emerging

Odisha has been developing a pharmaceutical and fine chemicals manufacturing base, particularly around Bhubaneswar and Cuttack. The state offers cost advantages for manufacturing, improving industrial infrastructure, and proximity to raw materials for certain chemical processes.

Several pharmaceutical companies have established manufacturing units in Odisha's industrial estates. The sector is small relative to established pharmaceutical manufacturing hubs like Gujarat and Andhra Pradesh, but is growing, and the state government has been actively pursuing investment in this sector.

## Technology and IT services — growing in Bhubaneswar

Bhubaneswar's IT sector has been expanding since STPI established its presence there in 1991. Companies including Infosys, TCS, Wipro, Tech Mahindra, and a number of mid-sized IT services companies have operations in the city. The sector employs a significant number of the city's engineering graduates and is growing as the talent pipeline from KIIT, IIT Bhubaneswar, and IIIT Bhubaneswar matures.

The growth in IT services is directly linked to the talent available in Bhubaneswar. As the talent base deepens, the scale and complexity of the work that companies are willing to locate in the city increases.

## Renewable energy — significant scale

Odisha has substantial potential for renewable energy — both solar, given the state's solar irradiance levels, and wind, given its long coastline. The state has been attracting investment in large-scale solar and wind projects, and the central government's renewable energy ambitions have produced policy frameworks that support this investment.

This is an early-stage growth story rather than an established industry, but the scale of renewable energy investment anticipated in Odisha over the next decade is significant.

## Tourism — underutilised but growing

Odisha has more tourism infrastructure and attraction than its visitor numbers reflect. Puri draws millions of domestic pilgrims annually to the Jagannath Temple. The Konark Sun Temple is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Chilika Lake ecosystem supports both birdwatching and dolphin tourism. The tribal cultural heritage of the state's interior — the Dongria Kondh, Bonda, and Kondh communities — is one of India's most distinctive but least-known cultural tourism assets.

International tourist arrivals in Odisha are low relative to the state's attractions, which indicates the tourism industry has significant room to grow as awareness improves and access becomes easier.

## The pattern

Odisha's growth is happening across multiple sectors simultaneously, which is unusual for a state of its development profile. The steel and resources base is expanding. The ports are becoming nationally significant. The technology sector is growing with the talent base. Pharmaceuticals are emerging. Renewables are starting.

The sectors are not yet deeply interconnected — Odisha does not yet have the industrial cluster dynamics that reinforce growth across adjacent sectors. But the breadth of sectors growing simultaneously creates a more resilient economic profile than single-sector dependence.
