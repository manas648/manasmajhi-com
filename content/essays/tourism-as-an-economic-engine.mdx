When people think about economic development, they usually think about industries.
Manufacturing.
Technology.
Infrastructure.
Exports.
Investment.
These sectors are important.
They create jobs.
Generate revenue.
Drive growth.
Yet there is another industry that often receives less attention than it deserves.
Tourism.
This is surprising because tourism possesses a unique characteristic.
It converts existing assets into economic opportunity.
A factory must be built.
A mine must be developed.
A technology platform must be created.
Tourism begins with something different.
A place.
A story.
A culture.
1

A landscape.
An experience.
The assets already exist.
The challenge is making them accessible.
This is why I believe tourism is one of Odisha's most underutilized economic opportunities.
The state possesses extraordinary advantages.
A coastline stretching hundreds of kilometers.
Ancient temples.
Rich tribal cultures.
Wildlife reserves.
Lakes.
Waterfalls.
Festivals.
Cuisine.
History.
Spiritual heritage.
Few places possess such diversity within a single state.
Yet many travelers know surprisingly little about Odisha.
That is the opportunity.
Tourism is ultimately an awareness business.
People visit places they know.
They recommend places they experience.
They return to places they enjoy.
2

The challenge is reducing the gap between what exists and what people know exists.
When I think about Odisha, I often imagine it through the eyes of a first-time visitor.
The experience can be remarkable.

That shift matters.
Because tourism changes narratives.
Economic development often begins with perception.
Investors evaluate perception.
Entrepreneurs evaluate perception.
Visitors evaluate perception.
Talent evaluates perception.
Tourism influences all of them.
A visitor who experiences a place develops a more nuanced understanding of it.
The destination becomes real.
Not abstract.
This creates economic value.
Tourism supports hotels.
3

Restaurants.
Transportation providers.
Tour guides.
Artisans.
Small businesses.
Local entrepreneurs.
Unlike many industries, tourism distributes economic activity across a wide range of participants.
The benefits extend beyond large organizations.
They reach communities.
Families.
Individuals.
This makes tourism particularly powerful.
A tourist does not spend money in a single location.
They spend across an ecosystem.
The economic impact multiplies.
There is another advantage.
Tourism encourages preservation.
Many forms of development consume resources.
Tourism often rewards protecting them.
Historic sites gain value when preserved.
Cultural traditions gain value when celebrated.
Natural environments gain value when maintained.
This creates alignment between economic incentives and cultural preservation.
4

That alignment is rare.
And valuable.
Infrastructure remains important.
Tourism cannot thrive without connectivity.
Roads matter.
Airports matter.
Railways matter.
Digital discoverability matters.
Hospitality standards matter.
Visitors remember experiences.
Every interaction contributes to the overall perception.
This is why tourism development requires systems thinking.
It is not simply about attractions.
It is about the entire visitor journey.
Discovery.
Access.
Experience.
Accommodation.
Transportation.
Storytelling.
Every element matters.
Technology is creating new opportunities here as well.
Travel decisions increasingly begin online.
5

A destination can reach a global audience through digital platforms.
Photos.
Videos.
Stories.
Recommendations.
The internet has dramatically reduced marketing barriers.
Places no longer need enormous budgets to gain visibility.
They need compelling stories and consistent execution.
Odisha possesses both stories and substance.
The challenge is connecting them to larger audiences.
What excites me most about tourism is its ability to create opportunity without requiring people to leave.
For decades, economic mobility often meant migration.
People moved toward opportunity.
Tourism allows opportunity to move toward people.
Communities can benefit from their existing assets.
Their culture.
Their history.
Their environment.
Their identity.
This creates a different model of development.
One rooted in local strengths.
One that generates both economic and cultural value.
When I imagine Odisha's future, tourism occupies an important place.
6

Not because tourism will solve every challenge.
No industry can.
But because tourism amplifies strengths that already exist.
It creates visibility.
Generates employment.
Supports entrepreneurship.
Strengthens local economies.
Preserves culture.
Changes perceptions.
Few industries achieve all of these simultaneously.
The world is increasingly searching for authentic experiences.
Odisha possesses many of them.
The opportunity is not creating something entirely new.
The opportunity is helping more people discover what already exists.
And sometimes, that is where the greatest opportunities are found.
Learning Outcome
Tourism is more than a travel industry. It is an economic engine that creates jobs, supports communities,
preserves culture, strengthens local economies, and transforms perceptions by converting existing assets
into opportunity.
