When people think about building global businesses, they usually picture places like Silicon Valley, New
York, London, or Singapore.
Few people picture Odisha.
Even fewer picture a student from an Odia-medium government school serving clients across multiple
countries.
Yet that is exactly what happened.
Not because it was easy.
Not because there was a blueprint.
Because the internet quietly changed the rules.
For most of human history, geography determined opportunity.
Where you were born mattered.
Where you lived mattered.
Who you knew mattered.
The world was organized around proximity.
Then something extraordinary happened.
Technology reduced distance.
A person sitting in Odisha could work with someone in California.
A founder in Bhubaneswar could advise a company in New York.
A recruiter in India could help a business in Europe hire a critical leader.
The barriers did not disappear.
But they became lower.
And that created opportunities that previous generations never had.
1

I did not fully appreciate this when I was younger.
Growing up, the world felt large.
Certain places felt important.
Certain people felt inaccessible.
Global business felt like something that happened somewhere else.
Over time, reality proved otherwise.
One client became two.
Two became five.
Five became many more.
Conversations that once felt impossible became normal.
The world became smaller.
Not physically.
Professionally.
What fascinated me most was not the technology itself.
It was what the technology enabled.
Access.
The ability to participate in conversations that geography would have prevented only a generation earlier.
The ability to compete based on value rather than location.
The ability to create opportunities from places the world often overlooks.
This is why I care deeply about regional development.
Not because every city must become the next Silicon Valley.
But because every region deserves a chance to participate in the global economy.
Talent exists everywhere.
2

Potential exists everywhere.
Ambition exists everywhere.
What is often missing is access.
Odisha is full of capable people.
Smart people.
Hardworking people.
Creative people.
People who can compete with anyone when given the opportunity.
The challenge has never been talent.
The challenge has been visibility.
For too long, many talented individuals have felt they needed to leave before they could succeed.
I understand why.
Opportunity often appears concentrated elsewhere.
But I believe the future looks different.
Remote work.
Global collaboration.
Artificial intelligence.
Digital infrastructure.
These forces are redistributing opportunity.
Not perfectly.
Not instantly.
But meaningfully.
The next generation will have advantages my generation never had.
3

A student with a laptop and an internet connection can now access knowledge, customers, mentors, and
opportunities that once required physical presence.
That changes everything.
My own journey is proof of that.
Not because it is extraordinary.
Because it is increasingly possible.
Building a global business from Odisha taught me something important.
Great ideas are not limited by geography.
Execution is not limited by geography.
Character is not limited by geography.
The only thing geography should influence is perspective.
And in many ways, I am grateful for mine.
Growing up away from traditional power centers teaches resourcefulness.
It teaches patience.
It teaches humility.
It teaches you how to build without assuming resources will magically appear.
Those lessons become advantages over time.
Today, when I think about success, I do not think only about companies.
I think about ecosystems.
Communities.
Regions.
The ability to create opportunities for others.
Because the ultimate goal is not simply to build a global business from Odisha.
4

It is to help make global ambition feel normal for the next generation growing up there.
One opportunity creates another.
One success story inspires another.
One bridge creates many more.
That is how ecosystems are built.
And that is how regions rise.
Learning Outcome
Geography may influence your starting point, but it does not have to determine your destination.
Opportunity expands when talented people gain access to global markets, networks, and ideas.
