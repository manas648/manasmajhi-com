There is a question I enjoy more than most people expect.
"What should I see in Odisha?"
Not because I have a prepared answer.
Because I get an opportunity to introduce someone to a place I care deeply about.
Every time friends from outside Odisha visit, I experience the state differently.
Through fresh eyes.
Places I have seen many times suddenly become new again.
Stories I take for granted become fascinating.
Experiences that feel ordinary reveal their uniqueness.
That is one of the gifts visitors provide.
They remind us to appreciate what is already around us.
If I were showing a friend around Odisha, I would not begin with a presentation.
Or statistics.
Or economic data.
I would begin with an experience.
Because Odisha is best understood through experience.
The first stop would almost certainly be Puri.
Not simply because of the sea.
And not simply because of the spiritual significance.
Puri captures something deeper.
A rhythm.

The experience of watching dolphins surface in the distance or seeing the horizon dissolve into water is
difficult to forget.
Places like Chilika remind us that beauty does not always need architecture.
Sometimes it only needs space.
Food would become part of the journey very quickly.
Because every region tells its story through food.
Odisha is no different.
I enjoy watching visitors discover flavors they were not expecting.
The simplicity.
The freshness.
The distinctiveness.
Food creates conversations.
And conversations create memories.
Often people remember meals long after they forget itineraries.
But if I am being honest, the places are only part of what I want visitors to experience.
The real Odisha lives in its people.
The shopkeeper who insists you stay for tea.
The local guide who shares stories beyond the official script.
The stranger who helps without expecting anything in return.
The family that treats guests like relatives.
Hospitality is difficult to measure.
Yet it shapes perception more than almost anything else.
Visitors often remember people more vividly than places.
3

I think that says something important.
If time allowed, I would show them parts of Odisha that rarely appear in travel brochures.
Small towns.
Local markets.
Rural landscapes.
Conversations with people who are building lives far from major cities.
Because every place contains two stories.
The story tourists see.
And the story residents live.
Both matter.
Understanding a place requires seeing both.
One of my favorite moments happens near the end of a visit.
It is usually subtle.
A comment.
An observation.
A realization.
Something along the lines of:
"This isn't what I expected."
I hear that often.
And I take it as a compliment.
Because expectations are frequently incomplete.
Many people know very little about Odisha before arriving.
They leave with a different understanding.
4

A richer understanding.
A more accurate understanding.
That transformation matters.
Tourism is not only about sightseeing.
It is about perspective.
Every visit creates a bridge between assumptions and reality.
Every conversation expands understanding.
Every experience changes perception.
That is why I enjoy showing people around Odisha.
Not because I want to convince them of anything.
Because I want them to experience it for themselves.
The history.
The culture.
The landscapes.
The food.
The people.
The stories.
The possibilities.
And perhaps most importantly, the feeling.
The feeling that this is a place with deep roots and a bright future.
A place that has much more to offer than most people realize.
A place I am proud to call home.
5

Learning Outcome
The best way to understand a place is to experience it through its people, stories, culture, and everyday
moments. Travel changes perceptions, and Odisha has a remarkable ability to surprise those willing to
explore it with curiosity.
